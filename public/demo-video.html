Sample

What it makes

Fernhouse is a tiny made-up plant-care app that comes in the download for practice. The skill wrote this script, filmed the screens and checked the result: 43 seconds, 1920×1080, with a Google voice and captions.

How it works

One sentence from you, six steps from Claude

  1. Checks your Mac

    Anything missing is explained one thing at a time, with the fix spelled out.

  2. Asks a few questions

    What the video is for, what to show off, how long, and which voice. It fills in the settings for you.

  3. Looks around your app

    A hidden browser visits each page without clicking anything, so the script uses your real button names.

  4. Writes a storyboard

    Every scene's picture and words, where the pointer goes, and how long the video will run.

  5. You approve the words

    Nothing is recorded until you say so. Change a spoken word afterwards and it asks you again.

  6. Makes and checks it

    Narration, camera moves, a pointer, captions. Then it checks the video and saves each new version beside the last.

Before you start

What you need

  • A Mac. It doesn't work on Windows yet.
  • Claude Code, opened in your app's project folder.
  • Node, the free program the tools run on. If you don't have it, the guide shows you how to install it from nodejs.org.
  • An app that opens in a web browser, on your computer or online.
  • A test account in your app, with tidy, made-up data. The hidden browser really signs in and clicks around, and anything in that account can end up in the video.
  • About an hour the first time, mostly installing and reading. It downloads about 300 MB of helpers once. After that, a new video takes about fifteen minutes.
  • Optional: a Google AI Studio key for a natural-sounding voice. It costs pennies per video, on your own account.
Get started

Four steps to your first video

The download includes a full guide (README.md) written for a first-time Mac user. This is the short version.

  1. Download the zip and double-click it in your Downloads folder. You'll get a folder called demo-video-skill.
  2. Open Claude Code in your app's project folder and say: Copy the demo-video skill folder from the demo-video-skill folder in my Downloads folder into this project's .claude/skills folder.
  3. Start Claude Code fresh so it notices the skill, then ask “Do you have the demo-video skill?” It should say yes.
  4. Say the sentence: Make a demo video of my app.
Good to know

Before you post your video

The free Mac voice is for practice

Apple's licence allows its built-in voices for personal, non-commercial use only. Practise with it, then switch to a Google voice for any video you publish, share publicly or use for a business. Only the voice changes.

Your passwords stay private

Your test account's password and any voice key go in a private file that Claude opens for you in TextEdit. Claude never reads it, and never asks for a password in the chat.

It won't press risky buttons

Delete, pay, send, invite, publish and sign-out buttons are refused. It can't know every app, though, which is why it needs a test account.

Nothing is uploaded

Your video stays on your Mac. Every version is kept (cut 1, cut 2…) and nothing is overwritten.

What it can't do yet

Phone apps from the App Store, Windows, and filming live motion. The video is made from still screens with smooth camera moves. For “Sign in with Google or Apple”, a window opens and you sign in once yourself.

Made for short videos

It's built for videos of 30 to 90 seconds: one idea per scene, with a title card and an end card in your app's name and colour.
